# Level 2 — Room 5: Split Containment / Volt Rescue

## 1. Room Identity

**Core identity:**  
Room 5 is a large cooperative puzzle in which Bob and Goop are physically separated into two different areas of the facility and must indirectly help each other rescue Volt.

Unlike Room 4, which is a fast reaction-based drone encounter, Room 5 should be slower, more deliberate, and puzzle-focused.

The main idea is:

> **Bob can traverse the containment chamber but cannot disable its security drones.  
> Goop can control the drone security networks but cannot enter Bob's traversal route.**

The player must therefore repeatedly switch between Bob and Goop:

1. Bob discovers which security network is blocking his path.
2. The player switches to Goop.
3. Goop changes which coloured drone network is disabled.
4. The player switches back to Bob.
5. Bob advances to the next safe position.
6. Repeat with increasingly complicated traversal.

The room ends with Bob reaching the mechanism that releases Volt.

---

# 2. Main Goals of Room 5

Room 5 should accomplish several things simultaneously:

- Give Level 2 a major final puzzle.
- Make slime switching essential rather than optional.
- Require Bob and Goop to solve different halves of the same problem.
- Create a strong visual objective by placing Volt in the middle of Bob's room.
- Reuse drones in a completely different way from Room 4.
- Reward observation and planning rather than shooting speed.
- Build anticipation around finally rescuing Volt.
- End Level 2 with all three slimes available.

The room should feel like:

**Separation → discovery → cooperation → increasingly complex routing → Volt rescue → reunion.**

---

# 3. Overall Room Structure

Room 5 is divided into three major sections:

### Room 5 Entry

The elevator arrives at the bottom.

An open maintenance vent becomes accessible beside the elevator.

Bob and Goop enter the same air duct.

The duct reaches a fork.

### Room 5.1 — Bob's Route

The left path climbs upward through a sticky-walled ventilation shaft.

Bob is the only slime capable of following this route.

It eventually opens into the large Volt containment chamber.

### Room 5.2 — Goop's Route

The right path descends through a contaminated ventilation duct.

The floor becomes covered in corrosive waste/acid that prevents Bob from following.

Goop travels downward into an abandoned sewer and eventually reaches the security-control area.

The two slimes remain physically separated while solving the main puzzle.

---

# 5. Elevator Arrival

Room 5 begins immediately after the Room 4 elevator encounter.

The elevator reaches the bottom.

The contrast should be noticeable.

Room 4 ends with:

- alarms,
- drones,
- firing,
- mechanical noise,
- rapid aiming,
- constant pressure.

Room 5 should initially become quieter.

The elevator brakes.

Remaining security activity above is cut off.

The elevator doors or side access area becomes available.

A maintenance vent on the side of the elevator shaft is visibly open.

This vent is the only obvious route forward.

---

# 6. Vent Entrance

The vent should be positioned so both Bob and Goop can enter normally.

It should not require Bob-specific movement yet.

The player needs to understand:

> Both slimes are entering the next area together.

Suggested environmental treatment:

- Damaged vent cover lying nearby.
- Broken bolts.
- Faint airflow.
- Maintenance lights inside.
- Pipes visible through gaps.
- Electrical cables hanging from the ceiling.
- Facility labels indicating maintenance access.

The vent should feel like a believable service route connecting the elevator machinery to containment infrastructure.

---

# 7. Shared Vent Section

The initial duct should be short.

Purpose:

- Give the player a short recovery after Room 4.
- Allow both slimes to move together briefly.
- Set up the upcoming separation.
- Avoid immediately dropping the player into another major encounter.

Suggested duration:

**10–20 seconds of movement maximum.**

Do not make this section a maze.

---

# 8. The Vent Fork

The shared duct reaches a very obvious two-way intersection.

One route goes left.

One route goes right.

The player should immediately understand that these routes correspond to different slime abilities.

No large tutorial text should be necessary.

---

# 9. Left Route — Bob

The left side should visually communicate:

> BOB ROUTE

The vent turns upward.

The internal wall surface becomes visibly sticky.

Possible visual language:

- Existing sticky surface material.
- Sticky tile markings.
- Familiar colour.
- Wet/adhesive appearance.
- Vertical climb clearly visible.

The shaft rises steeply enough that Goop cannot follow.

Bob climbs upward.

---

# 10. Right Route — Goop

The right route should communicate:

> GOOP ROUTE

Shortly after entering:

- The duct slopes downward.
- Corrosive liquid appears across the floor.
- The contamination becomes progressively heavier.
- Bob cannot safely cross it.
- Goop can.

This establishes a hard separation.

The player understands that Goop must continue alone.

---

# 11. Separation Rule

Once the player commits each slime to its route, they should not be able to simply bring the other slime around through an exploit.

Bob's route requires:

- wall adhesion,
- vertical climbing,
- sticky traversal.

Goop's route requires:

- corrosion resistance / compatibility with the hazardous sewer route.

The routes should remain separated until Volt is rescued.

---

# 12. Separation Should Feel Intentional

The game should not simply create an invisible wall saying:

> Bob cannot enter.

Instead, the level geometry explains the limitation.

Bob physically cannot descend safely through Goop's contaminated path.

Goop physically cannot climb Bob's sticky vertical shaft.

This preserves the game's environmental design language.

---

# 13. Checkpoint Before the Split

Place a paired checkpoint near the vent intersection.

The checkpoint should record both slime positions.

Suggested respawn:

- Bob near the beginning of the left path.
- Goop near the beginning of the right path.

This prevents the player from replaying Room 4 after dying in Room 5.

Room 4 should be considered completely finished once the elevator reaches the bottom.

---

# 14. Room 5.1 — Bob's Containment Chamber

Bob climbs through the left ventilation shaft.

After a short ascent, he reaches an opening overlooking a large containment chamber.

This is the primary visual space of Room 5.

The first thing the player should notice is:

> **Volt.**

---

# 15. Volt as the Central Landmark

Volt is contained inside a glass box suspended prominently in the centre of the room.

All major faces of the containment box are glass.

The container hangs from the ceiling using:

- mechanical support arms,
- reinforced cables,
- containment rails,
- or a large ceiling-mounted apparatus.

Volt should remain visible throughout much of Bob's traversal.

This gives Bob a constant objective.

---

# 16. Volt's Presentation

Volt should look alive but imprisoned.

Possible presentation:

- Faint cyan/electric glow.
- Occasional internal electrical pulses.
- Small arcs against the containment hardware.
- Light reflecting from nearby metal.
- Volt moving slightly inside.
- Containment monitors showing active specimen status.

Volt should provide one of the strongest light sources in the chamber.

The room should visually draw the player toward the centre.

---

# 17. Chamber Scale

The containment chamber should be fairly large and vertical.

It must support:

- wall climbing,
- ledge traversal,
- sticky surfaces,
- jumps,
- charged jumps,
- possible ceiling movement,
- drone sightlines,
- multiple elevations,
- safe resting positions.

The player should feel like Bob is navigating around a secure research installation built specifically to prevent access to Volt.

---

# 18. Bob's Starting Position

Bob exits the ventilation opening onto a small secure platform.

This area contains:

- a checkpoint,
- a clear view of Volt,
- a clear view of the first security drone,
- enough room to switch slimes safely.

Bob should not immediately be under attack while the player is still orienting themselves.

---

# 19. Bob's Checkpoint

A checkpoint should activate shortly after Bob enters Room 5.1.

At approximately the same progression point, Goop should eventually receive a corresponding safe-state checkpoint when reaching the control area.

The checkpoint system must understand that the slimes are in different locations.

On death/reset:

- Bob returns to his containment-room checkpoint.
- Goop returns to the appropriate sewer/control checkpoint.
- Security network state resets.
- Drones reset.
- Volt remains contained.
- Parkour state resets.

---

# 20. First Drone

Slightly ahead of Bob's checkpoint is the first coloured security drone.

This drone is:

**RED.**

Its purpose is to teach the entire cooperative puzzle.

The drone should directly block Bob's only route forward.

There should be no clever way around it.

---

# 21. First Red Drone Behaviour

The Red drone should have:

- A clearly red emissive eye/core.
- A visible red marking or light around its mounting point.
- A predictable security-facing direction.
- A firing/targeting zone covering Bob's next traversal section.

If Bob tries to progress:

- The drone locks onto him.
- It begins firing.
- The route should be effectively unsafe.

The intended conclusion is:

> Bob cannot solve this himself.

---

# 22. Coloured Drone Rule

Room 5 introduces three security networks:

- **RED**
- **BLUE**
- **GREEN**

Every security drone belongs to exactly one network.

The drone's colour determines which Goop switch controls it.

---

# 23. Important Drone Rule

These Room 5 security drones **cannot be destroyed by Goop**.

They are not combat targets.

They function as puzzle hazards.

Goop controls them indirectly through the security switches.

This deliberately differentiates Room 5 from Room 4.

---

# 24. Drone Visual Language

Each security drone should visibly communicate its network.

Minimum:

- Red eye / Blue eye / Green eye.

Better:

### RED

- Red emissive eye.
- Triangle symbol.

### BLUE

- Blue emissive eye.
- Circle symbol.

### GREEN

- Green emissive eye.
- Square symbol.

Using both colour and shape improves readability.

---

# 25. Drone Disabled State

When a network is disabled:

All drones associated with that network should visibly shut down.

Possible behaviour:

- Eye goes dark.
- Drone head drops slightly.
- Tracking stops.
- Weapon folds inward.
- Motor sound stops.
- Small shutdown sound.
- Associated environment lights dim.

The player should immediately understand:

> This drone is safe.

---

# 26. Drone Reactivation

When Goop changes the disabled network, previously disabled drones reactivate.

Example:

Initial:

RED — ONLINE  
BLUE — ONLINE  
GREEN — ONLINE

Goop disables RED:

RED — OFFLINE  
BLUE — ONLINE  
GREEN — ONLINE

Goop then disables BLUE:

RED — ONLINE  
BLUE — OFFLINE  
GREEN — ONLINE

Goop disables GREEN:

RED — ONLINE  
BLUE — ONLINE  
GREEN — OFFLINE

Only **one network may be disabled at a time**.

---

# 27. Why Only One Network Can Be Disabled

This is the key rule that turns the system into a puzzle.

If Goop could disable all three permanently, the player would simply:

1. Disable Red.
2. Disable Blue.
3. Disable Green.
4. Walk through the entire room.

There would be no puzzle.

Instead, changing the disabled network changes which parts of Bob's route are currently safe.

---

# 28. Room 5.2 — Goop's Sewer

While Bob is blocked by the first Red drone, the player switches to Goop.

Goop continues along the right-side duct.

The duct slopes downward.

The passage becomes increasingly damaged and contaminated.

Eventually Goop exits into the sewer.

---

# 29. Sewer Identity

The sewer should contrast heavily with Bob's containment chamber.

Bob's environment:

- clean industrial containment,
- tall,
- open,
- security systems,
- controlled lighting.

Goop's environment:

- dirty,
- wet,
- claustrophobic,
- damaged,
- abandoned,
- pipes,
- sewage,
- corrosion.

This helps switching between the two slimes feel visually distinct.

---

# 30. Sewer Geometry

The sewer should remain relatively simple.

Potential elements:

- Concrete/metal channel.
- Large pipes.
- Flowing contaminated liquid.
- Maintenance lights.
- Rust.
- Broken equipment.
- Grates.
- Dripping water.
- Steam.
- Dead machinery.

Goop should never become lost.

The sewer is a route to the control room, not a separate maze.

---

# 31. Sewer Starting End

Behind Goop's starting route, the sewer should visibly terminate in a large circular tunnel blocked by metal bars/grating.

This is mostly environmental.

The barred circular opening communicates that this sewer network extends beyond the playable space.

It also prevents the area from looking like a conveniently sealed hallway.

---

# 32. Single Broken Drone

Room 5.2 contains exactly **one damaged drone**.

This drone exists primarily for:

- immersion,
- tension,
- environmental storytelling,
- a short surprise.

It is not intended to become another combat gauntlet.

---

# 33. Why This Drone Is Different

The damaged sewer drone must look obviously different from intact security drones.

It should have:

- Broken armour.
- Missing panels.
- Exposed electronics.
- Rust.
- Water damage.
- Sparks.
- Damaged weapon housing.
- Possibly one broken rotor/mount.
- Exposed vulnerable core.

This explains why Goop can destroy this specific drone.

---

# 34. Consistency Rule

Goop still **cannot destroy normal intact drones**.

The sewer drone is vulnerable because it is already heavily damaged.

Goop is attacking exposed internal components, not dissolving normal drone armour.

This distinction should be visually obvious.

---

# 35. Broken Drone Initial State

The drone initially appears completely inactive.

Possible positioning:

- Lying partly in shallow corrosive water.
- Collapsed against a pipe.
- Leaning against the sewer wall.
- Half-buried under debris.

Its eye is dark.

No weapon tracking.

No obvious threat.

The player may initially assume it is simply environmental decoration.

---

# 36. Activation Radius

When Goop enters a defined radius:

The damaged drone begins rebooting.

Suggested radius:

Large enough that the player can clearly see the drone before triggering it.

Do not activate the drone from around a blind corner.

---

# 37. 1.5-Second Reboot

Once triggered:

### 0.0 s

A spark or electrical crack.

### 0.2 s

Drone eye flickers.

### 0.4 s

Servos begin moving.

### 0.7 s

Weapon assembly starts raising.

### 1.0 s

Targeting light appears intermittently.

### 1.3 s

Strong warning tone.

### ~1.5 s

Drone becomes fully operational and begins firing.

---

# 38. Goop Response

Goop should shoot the exposed vulnerable component before activation completes.

One good hit should be enough.

The point is reaction and atmosphere, not sustained combat.

Successful shot:

- Acid/effect strikes exposed component.
- Electrical short occurs.
- Drone violently sparks.
- Power dies.
- Drone falls completely limp.

---

# 39. Failure to Destroy Drone Quickly

If the player is too slow:

The drone becomes active.

It can fire at Goop.

However, it should still remain destroyable via the exposed core.

The encounter must not become extremely punishing.

The player should be able to:

- dodge,
- aim,
- destroy the drone,
- continue.

---

# 40. No Additional Sewer Drones

Do not add:

- 2-drone wave.
- 3-drone wave.
- 5-drone wave.

Room 4 already performs the escalating drone combat role.

The single sewer drone is enough.

After it is destroyed, the focus shifts entirely toward the cooperative puzzle.

---

# 41. Sewer Transition After Drone

Following the broken drone:

Goop travels a short additional distance.

The sewer begins transitioning into:

- maintenance infrastructure,
- security cables,
- control conduits,
- cleaner mechanical architecture.

This visually signals that Goop is approaching something important.

---

# 42. Security Control Area

Goop eventually reaches a secure maintenance/control position.

This is Goop's destination for the main Room 5 puzzle.

The player should immediately notice three controls.

---

# 43. The Three Security Switches

There are three clearly distinguished controls:

- RED
- BLUE
- GREEN

Goop activates them using the acid projectile / shooting mechanic.

The switches should be positioned close enough together that changing network does not require tedious movement.

---

# 44. Switch Layout

Recommended arrangement:

              [ RED ]

        [ BLUE ]   [ GREEN ]

Or:

[ RED ]   [ BLUE ]   [ GREEN ]

The exact visual arrangement is less important than readability.

The player should be able to aim quickly between them.

---

# 45. Switch Visual Design

Each control should use:

- Strong colour.
- Matching drone symbol.
- Clear ON/OFF state.
- Indicator light.
- Cable/conduit leading into the facility.

Example:

RED
△
SECURITY NETWORK
ONLINE

When disabled:

RED
△
SECURITY NETWORK
OFFLINE

---

# 46. How Goop Activates Switches

Goop shoots a control.

The projectile impact should cause a deliberate system reaction.

For example:

1. Acid strikes exposed control contact.
2. Electrical surge.
3. Current network shuts down.
4. Previously disabled network reactivates.
5. Lights update.
6. Drone states update.

This keeps Goop's acid mechanic integrated into the puzzle.

---

# 47. Switch Response Must Be Fast

Network changes should happen quickly.

Suggested:

**0.25–0.75 seconds.**

Do not make the player wait through long animations every time they change colour.

Switching networks will happen repeatedly.

---

# 48. Control Station Feedback

When Goop changes networks, give strong feedback.

Visual:

- Selected switch dims.
- Other switches relight.
- Security schematic updates.
- Corresponding conduit changes state.

Audio:

- Electrical shutdown.
- Network tone.
- Remote machinery powering down.
- Reactivation sound for old network.

---

# 49. Optional Security Display

A nearby screen or physical panel can display:

SECURITY GRID

RED     OFFLINE  
BLUE    ONLINE  
GREEN   ONLINE

This is useful but should not be required to understand the puzzle.

The switches and drones themselves should already communicate state.

---

# 50. Teaching the First Network Change

At this point Bob is blocked by the Red drone.

Goop reaches the switches.

The RED switch should be the most obvious initial solution.

Potential assistance:

- Red switch blinking.
- Red security conduit active.
- Brief objective:

> DISABLE RED SECURITY NETWORK

Goop shoots RED.

---

# 51. First Cooperative Payoff

Immediately after RED is disabled:

Back in Bob's room:

- Red drone eye goes dark.
- Weapon drops.
- Targeting beam disappears.
- Route becomes safe.

The player switches back to Bob.

Bob can now progress.

This teaches:

> What Goop does in Room 5.2 changes Bob's Room 5.1.

---

# 52. Bob's Parkour Philosophy

Bob's parkour should not simply be random difficult jumps.

Every section should combine:

- Bob's movement abilities.
- A security colour.
- A safe waiting location.
- A clear next objective.

The security puzzle and traversal puzzle should be designed together.

---

# 53. Important Design Rule

Every time Bob requires Goop to change the disabled colour:

**Bob must have a safe place to wait.**

The player is controlling one slime at a time.

Therefore Bob cannot be expected to dodge an active drone while the player is operating Goop.

---

# 54. Safe Zones

Safe zones can be:

- Recessed maintenance alcoves.
- Concrete walls.
- Thick containment supports.
- Small covered ledges.
- Vent recesses.
- Shielded machinery platforms.

When Bob reaches one:

- Nearby active drones cannot hit him.
- The player can safely switch to Goop.
- The next required drone colour is visible.

---

# 55. Avoid Artificial Safe Zones

Safe spots should feel like natural architecture.

Do not place obvious floating "switching platforms."

Instead design the chamber so structural cover creates safe positions naturally.

---

# 56. Bob Parkour Beat 1 — Red Tutorial

Starting platform.

Red drone blocks the only path.

Goop disables RED.

Bob crosses.

This section should be mechanically easy.

Example traversal:

- Normal jump.
- Short sticky-wall climb.
- Safe ledge.

The focus is learning the network rule.

---

# 57. Beat 1 Completion

Bob reaches a protected alcove.

From this position:

- The first Red drone is behind him.
- A BLUE drone clearly controls the next section.
- Bob cannot proceed safely.

This teaches the next rule:

> Disabling another colour will reactivate Red.

---

# 58. Bob Parkour Beat 2 — Blue

Player switches to Goop.

Goop shoots BLUE.

State becomes:

RED — ONLINE  
BLUE — OFFLINE  
GREEN — ONLINE

The first Red drone powers back on.

But Bob is already behind cover.

This is very important.

The player sees that previous networks really do reactivate.

---

# 59. Blue Traversal

Bob proceeds through a route controlled by Blue drones.

Potential traversal concepts:

- Sticky vertical wall climb.
- Sideways transition between two sticky surfaces.
- Jump to a narrow maintenance platform.
- Drop onto a protected ledge.

The exact geometry can be designed by Astra/Codex.

---

# 60. Bob Parkour Beat 3 — Green

Bob reaches another safe area.

A GREEN drone controls the next path.

Player switches to Goop.

Goop disables GREEN.

Blue reactivates behind Bob.

Bob proceeds.

---

# 61. Green Traversal Possibilities

This section can escalate Bob's movement slightly.

Potential mechanics:

- Ceiling sticky traversal.
- Charged jump to a wall.
- Wall release into landing.
- Bounce-assisted height gain.
- Crossing an exposed gap between safe structures.

Again, the security layout should matter.

---

# 62. Middle Puzzle Escalation

After teaching Red → Blue → Green individually, the room should stop being purely linear.

The player now understands the rules.

The next section should require actual planning.

---

# 63. Mixed Security Puzzle

The central portion of Bob's route should contain multiple coloured drones visible from different positions.

Example:

- Red drone covers one sticky wall.
- Blue drone covers an upper bridge.
- Green drone covers a ceiling crossing.

Bob must move from safe pocket to safe pocket while Goop changes the disabled network.

---

# 64. Example Mixed Sequence

Possible solution:

1. Disable RED.
2. Bob crosses first exposed platform.
3. Bob reaches cover.
4. Disable BLUE.
5. Bob climbs blue-controlled wall.
6. Bob reaches upper alcove.
7. Disable GREEN.
8. Bob traverses ceiling.
9. Bob reaches structural column.
10. Disable RED again.
11. Bob crosses final section.

The exact sequence can change during greyboxing.

The important part is that colours must be revisited.

---

# 65. Reusing Colours

Do not design the room as simply:

RED → BLUE → GREEN → END.

That is too obvious.

After teaching all three, the player should need to return to a previous colour.

For example:

RED → BLUE → GREEN → RED → BLUE.

This demonstrates that the player understands the system rather than merely following a predetermined colour order.

---

# 66. Drone Placement Philosophy

Drones should be placed to control specific traversal spaces.

Examples:

- Watching a wall.
- Watching a bridge.
- Watching a landing zone.
- Watching a ceiling route.
- Watching an exposed jump arc.

Their sightlines should define the puzzle.

---

# 67. Drone Facing

The drones can either:

- remain mostly fixed at authored angles,

or

- slowly scan through small predefined arcs.

Do not make them freely rotate 360° and unpredictably cover the entire room.

Their danger zones must be learnable.

---

# 68. Recommended Drone Behaviour

Preferred:

Each drone has:

- Base facing direction.
- Small scanning arc.
- Defined detection cone.
- Short target-acquisition warning.
- Rapid fire once Bob remains exposed.

This makes them feel alive while preserving puzzle readability.

---

# 69. Drone Line-of-Sight Visualisation

The player should be able to understand where drones are dangerous.

Possible presentation:

- Subtle coloured search beam.
- Visible targeting cone.
- Scanning light on surfaces.
- Coloured projected dot.
- Volumetric-looking beam if performance permits.

Do not make the player guess invisible detection volumes.

---

# 70. Searchlight Colour

A drone's scanning light should match its network.

Red drone:

- Red scanner.

Blue drone:

- Blue scanner.

Green drone:

- Green scanner.

When disabled:

- Scanner disappears.

---

# 71. Avoiding Visual Overload

Do not flood the entire chamber with giant saturated beams.

Keep them:

- narrow,
- readable,
- purposeful.

Volt's cyan glow should remain the strongest central visual landmark.

---

# 72. Bob's Movement Abilities to Showcase

Room 5.1 is a good opportunity to combine Bob's mastered abilities.

Possible use:

- Sticky walls.
- Sticky ceilings.
- Charged jumps.
- Controlled drops.
- Bounce behaviour.
- Wall-to-platform transitions.
- Vent movement.

However, no brand-new Bob mechanic should be introduced this late in the room.

This is mastery, not another tutorial.

---

# 73. Astra/Codex Parkour Autonomy

Astra/Codex should be allowed to design the exact greybox parkour layout.

However, it must follow strict design constraints.

It should **not** invent new mechanics.

---

# 74. Astra/Codex Parkour Constraints

The generated parkour must:

- Build around Bob's existing movement mechanics.
- Use the established sticky-surface visual language.
- Keep Volt visible regularly.
- Include safe switching zones.
- Integrate Red/Blue/Green drone sightlines.
- Require several network changes.
- Reuse colours after their first tutorial.
- Avoid impossible blind jumps.
- Avoid extremely precise jumps.
- Avoid long periods of simply climbing vertically.
- Avoid locations where Bob can bypass drones.
- Keep camera collision usable.
- Preserve clear progression toward Volt.
- End at the Volt release mechanism.

---

# 75. Astra/Codex Should Design for Readability First

The parkour should have:

- obvious destinations,
- readable silhouettes,
- visible safe zones,
- clear sticky surfaces,
- predictable landing areas.

Do not prioritise complexity over understanding.

---

# 76. Vertical Progression

Bob's route should gradually move closer to Volt.

A useful layout could spiral around the containment box.

For example:

Start:

Outer lower wall.

Then:

Mid-level scaffolding.

Then:

Upper maintenance supports.

Then:

Containment apparatus.

Then:

Volt release platform.

This gives Bob's journey visible physical progress.

---

# 77. Volt as Navigation

Volt should help orient the player.

Even if Bob turns around or climbs vertically:

The glowing glass containment box provides a constant reference point.

The player should rarely wonder:

> Where am I supposed to be going?

They know the objective is ultimately Volt.

---

# 78. Goop's Role During Main Puzzle

Once Goop reaches the switches, Goop mostly remains in the control area.

This is intentional.

Goop's gameplay becomes:

- Observe Bob's current problem.
- Switch security network.
- Confirm network state.
- Allow Bob to proceed.

Goop is acting as the remote operator.

---

# 79. Avoid Making Goop Tedious

Because Goop repeatedly returns to the same controls:

- Keep switches close together.
- Keep spawn/checkpoint near the switches.
- Do not require extra traversal between every colour change.
- Make switching responsive.
- Do not play long animations.

The intellectual decision is the interesting part.

Running between controls is not.

---

# 80. Optional View of Bob's Room

If geometry allows, Goop's control area may have:

- A reinforced observation window.
- A security camera display.
- A schematic of the containment chamber.

This is optional.

Because the game is single-player, the player already knows Bob's situation from controlling him.

Do not build a complicated surveillance system unless it adds meaningful presentation value.

---

# 81. Recommended Control Room Atmosphere

The control space should look partially functional despite the sewer around it.

Visuals:

- Old terminals.
- Coloured conduits.
- Warning displays.
- Electrical boxes.
- Facility labels.
- Pipes transitioning into security cabling.
- View toward the containment infrastructure if possible.

The player should feel that Goop has found the maintenance controls underneath the main chamber.

---

# 82. Security Network Architecture

Conceptually, use one central controller:

`SecurityNetworkController`

It owns:

- active disabled colour,
- registered drones,
- switches,
- visual indicators,
- reset state.

Possible enum:

`NONE`
`RED`
`BLUE`
`GREEN`

When Goop activates a switch:

`disabledNetwork = selectedColour`

Every drone evaluates:

`isDisabled = drone.networkColour === disabledNetwork`

# 83. No Per-Drone Custom Logic for Switches

Avoid wiring each individual switch manually to every drone.

All drones should register with the network controller.

This makes the system scalable and easier to debug.

# 84. Security Switch Behaviour

Conceptually:

`activateNetworkDisable(RED)`

results in:

- Red drones disable.
- Blue drones enable.
- Green drones enable.
- Red switch displays OFFLINE.
- Blue switch displays ONLINE.
- Green switch displays ONLINE.

The same system handles all three colours.

When Goop reactivates a network by changing colours, consider a very short power-up delay.

It should not be long enough for Bob to exploit by sprinting through entire sections.

1. Player switches to Goop.

3. Bob becomes exposed while inactive.
4. Bob is killed before the player can switch back.

That would feel unfair.

# 151. Room 5 Design Summary

**Room type:**  
Split-character cooperative puzzle / traversal finale.

**Main objective:**  
Rescue Volt.

**Slimes required:**  
Bob + Goop.

**Room 5.1:**  
Bob's large Volt containment chamber.

**Room 5.2:**  
Goop's contaminated sewer and security-control route.

**Separation mechanic:**  
Bob uses the sticky vertical vent. Goop uses the hazardous sewer route.

**Goop sewer combat:**  
Exactly **one damaged drone**, primarily for atmosphere.

**Damaged drone rule:**  
It can be destroyed because its internal components are exposed.

**Normal drone rule:**  
Cannot be destroyed.

**Security networks:**

- Red
- Blue
- Green

**Core network rule:**  
Only one colour can be disabled at any moment.

**Bob gameplay:**  
Parkour between safe positions while coloured security drones control sections of the route.

**Goop gameplay:**  
Operate Red/Blue/Green switches remotely.

**Main puzzle loop:**

Bob identifies blocked route  
→ switch to Goop  
→ disable required network  
→ previous network reactivates  
→ switch to Bob  
→ move to next safe zone  
→ repeat.

**Parkour requirement:**  
Astra/Codex may design the exact greybox layout, but it must build around existing Bob mechanics, safe zones, drone sightlines, repeated colour switching, and a clear progression toward Volt.

**Central visual objective:**  
Volt suspended in an all-glass containment box in the centre of Room 5.1.

**Final interaction:**  
Bob reaches and activates a physical release mechanism.

**Rescue result:**

- Security drones permanently shut down.
- Volt containment opens.
- Volt joins the slime roster.
- Bob and Goop routes reconnect.
- All three slimes reunite.

**Volt introduction:**  
One very small electrical interaction opens the exit.

**Main design principle:**  

> Room 4 tests reaction speed.  
> Room 5 tests cooperation, planning, switching and mastery of Bob's traversal.

**Desired final feeling:**  

The player should finish Room 5 feeling that Bob and Goop genuinely needed each other to rescue Volt, rather than simply completing two unrelated paths.
