## Room 1 — Containment Chamber

Design the opening room of **Speciman** as a small, sterile containment chamber inside an abandoned research laboratory.

### Environment and Visual Style

The room should initially feel **clean, clinical, empty, and controlled**.

- The walls, floor, and ceiling are covered in **clean white laboratory tiles/panels**.
- Use bright fluorescent lighting to give the room a sterile research-facility appearance.
- The room should be mostly empty so that the player's attention is immediately drawn toward the centre.
- Avoid unnecessary furniture or clutter. This is a containment chamber rather than a normal laboratory workspace.

In the **centre of the room**, place a raised laboratory pedestal.

On top of the pedestal is a **transparent glass containment box**.

Inside the glass box is a single **egg containing the player character, a small blue slime**.

### Opening Cutscene

The level begins with a short in-engine cutscene focused on the containment box.

1. The camera establishes the empty laboratory room and slowly focuses on the glass containment chamber.
2. The egg begins to crack.
3. The egg hatches and a small blue slime emerges.
4. The slime briefly looks around and realises that it is trapped inside the glass box.
5. It begins pushing against the inside of the container.
6. The glass box shifts toward the edge of the pedestal.
7. The box falls from the pedestal and shatters against the floor.
8. The slime lands safely among the broken glass.

The camera then transitions into normal gameplay and gives control to the player.

### Player Objective

The player's immediate objective is simple:

**Escape the containment room.**

There is a normal laboratory door on one side of the room, but the door is completely closed.

The slime cannot operate door handles or conventional laboratory controls, making the obvious exit inaccessible.

The player therefore needs to find another way out.

### Sticky Wall Mechanic

On the **same wall as the locked door**, place an open ventilation shaft near the top of the room.

The vent should be far too high for the slime to reach using ordinary movement.

This wall introduces the player's first important ability: **wall adhesion**.

Most of the room uses normal white laboratory tiles that the slime **cannot stick to**.

However, a section of the wall leading toward the ventilation shaft is covered in visibly damaged **sticky tiles**.

These tiles should:

- Have a **yellow-green / sickly green tint**.
- Look chemically stained, corroded, or contaminated.
- Clearly contrast with the clean white tiles covering the rest of the chamber.
- Form a readable path from the floor toward the ventilation shaft.

Only these marked surfaces should allow the slime to stick and climb.

The player should naturally notice the unusual wall, experiment with it, discover that the slime can adhere to it, and then climb toward the vent.

The room should teach this mechanic through **visual design and player experimentation rather than text instructions wherever possible**.

### Vent and Transition to Room 2

The ventilation shaft should already be open, with enough space for the slime to enter.

Once the player climbs the sticky wall and enters the vent, they move into a **small enclosed air duct**.

The air duct should:

- Be narrow and claustrophobic compared with the containment room.
- Continue the laboratory's metallic ventilation-system aesthetic.
- Be simple and mostly linear so that the player cannot become lost.
- Lead downward toward the next area.

At the end of the duct is an opening in the floor.

The slime moves through the opening and **falls out of the ventilation system into Room 2**, creating a natural transition into the next gameplay area.

### Intended Gameplay Flow

The room should communicate the following sequence without requiring complicated instructions:

**Hatch → Escape the glass container → Investigate the locked door → Notice the unusual wall → Discover wall adhesion → Climb to the vent → Crawl through the air duct → Fall into Room 2.**

The main purpose of Room 1 is to establish the laboratory setting, introduce the player character, and teach the player that **certain visually marked surfaces can be climbed while ordinary surfaces cannot**.
