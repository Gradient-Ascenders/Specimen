## Room 2 — Bounce Training Chamber

Design the second gameplay space of Level 1 as a room that teaches the player the slime’s **big jumping / bounce mechanic**.

### Context and Transition

After escaping Room 1 through the ventilation shaft, the slime crawls through a narrow air duct and eventually reaches an opening at the bottom.

The slime drops out of the vent and falls a noticeable distance into **another chamber entirely**.

This is **not** the room directly adjacent to Room 1. It should feel like the slime has dropped deeper into another section of the facility.

As the slime falls, the player gets their **first glimpse of the bounce mechanic**:

- The slime hits the floor,
- compresses dramatically,
- then rebounds upward in a large, exaggerated bounce.

This should immediately communicate that the slime is not just able to move and climb, but also able to **store force and launch itself upward**.

### Environment and Visual Style

This room should still belong to the same research facility, but it should feel slightly different from Room 1 so that progression is clear.

- The room is larger and taller than the first room.
- It still uses clean laboratory materials such as white tiles, reinforced wall panels, and industrial metal framing.
- The room should feel like a **test chamber** or **vertical movement chamber** rather than a containment cell.
- Lighting remains bright and clinical, but there can be subtle warning colours or test markings to make it feel more mechanical and experimental.

The room should visually suggest that it is designed around **height and movement**.

### Core Purpose

The purpose of this room is to teach the player:

- that the slime can perform **large upward bounces**,
- that bouncing can be used intentionally to reach higher places,
- and that some obstacles cannot be solved by climbing alone.

This room should act as a tutorial space where the player learns the bounce mechanic through level design and experimentation rather than heavy text explanation.

### Layout Overview

The room should be designed as a **vertical tutorial challenge** with a clear sequence of learning.

#### 1. Entry Drop Zone

The player falls from the vent into a safe landing area at the bottom of the room.

- The landing should naturally cause a visible bounce.
- The floor here should be open and uncluttered so the player can observe and feel the rebound.
- This is the first moment where the player notices that falls and jumps have a springy quality.

This first bounce is not yet a puzzle — it is just a demonstration.

#### 2. First Bounce Lesson — Small Raised Platform

Near the landing area, place a ledge or platform that is slightly too high to reach with normal movement alone, but easy to reach with a jump or bounce.

- This should be the simplest test.
- The player should quickly realise they need to use the bounce mechanic to get onto it.
- The distance should be forgiving so the player learns the mechanic without frustration.

This teaches the basic idea:
**bounce to gain height.**

#### 3. Second Bounce Lesson — Wider Gap

After the first platform, introduce a small horizontal gap between two raised sections.

- The player must bounce across the gap.
- This teaches that the mechanic is not only for vertical height, but also for crossing distance.
- The gap should be safe enough that failure simply drops the player back to the previous area for an immediate retry.

This reinforces:
**bounce to travel farther.**

#### 4. Combined Lesson — Bounce Then Climb

Next, introduce a section where the player must use both mechanics together.

- The slime must bounce onto a higher surface,
- then climb a marked sticky wall section to continue upward.

This is important because it shows that the room is not replacing the climbing mechanic — it is expanding on it.

The player learns:
**movement abilities can be chained together.**

#### 5. Main Puzzle Beat — Vertical Bounce Shaft

The centrepiece of the room should be a taller vertical section or shaft.

This shaft contains several staggered ledges or wall features that force the player to bounce upward in stages.

Possible layout features:

- one low platform,
- then a higher offset ledge,
- then a sticky wall section,
- then another jump to a final platform.

This sequence should feel like a short “bounce ascent” challenge.

The player should feel that they are now deliberately mastering the mechanic rather than just discovering it.

#### 6. Exit Platform and Door / Vent

At the top or far end of the room, place the exit.

This could be:

- a vent leading onward,
- an open maintenance passage,
- or a doorway into the next chamber.

The exit should only be reachable once the player has successfully used the bounce mechanic throughout the room.

### Suggested Spatial Structure

A good overall structure for the room is:

- **Bottom landing zone**
- **Low tutorial platform**
- **Gap-crossing section**
- **Bounce + climb section**
- **Vertical shaft / multi-step ascent**
- **Exit ledge**

This creates a clear difficulty curve:
**see bounce → try bounce → use bounce intentionally → combine bounce with climb → complete a full traversal challenge using bounce.**

### Fail State and Player Comfort

Because this is a teaching room, failure should be low-friction.

- Falling should reset the player only a short distance back, not all the way to the start.
- The room should avoid punishing hazards.
- Missed jumps should mostly lead to quick retries rather than major setbacks.
- The player should feel encouraged to experiment.

### Visual Communication

To make the mechanic readable:

- Use height differences that clearly invite upward movement.
- Use strong framing so the player can see the next ledge above them.
- Use sticky wall tiles only where climbing is intended.
- Make jump targets visually distinct so the player can read the route.

If you want, the room can also include subtle environmental details such as:

- test markings on floors,
- bounce-related hazard striping,
- lab observation windows,
- or broken experimental infrastructure suggesting the facility once tested mobility or containment.

### Intended Player Experience

The room should create the following experience:

1. The player drops in and accidentally performs a big bounce.
2. The player notices that this bounce can be controlled and used.
3. The player uses bounce to reach a higher platform.
4. The player uses bounce to cross a gap.
5. The player combines bounce with climbing.
6. The player completes a short vertical traversal challenge.
7. The player exits having learned that bouncing is now a core part of movement.

### Summary of Room Identity

**Room 2 is a vertical laboratory test chamber that teaches the player the slime’s jump / big-bounce mechanic through safe, readable traversal challenges.**

Its role is to expand movement mastery after Room 1:
- **Room 1 teaches climbing.**
- **Room 2 teaches bouncing.**
- **Together, they prepare the player to combine both mechanics in later rooms.**
