## Room 3 — Precision Traversal and Laser Hazard

Design **Room 3** as the first major challenge space after the tutorial-style rooms. The player exits Room 2 through an open door and enters a much larger chamber that tests their understanding of the slime’s movement abilities in a more demanding and punishing way.

### Core Purpose

Room 3 marks the transition from **learning mechanics** to **applying them under pressure**.

This room should test the player’s ability to:

- use **big bounces** deliberately,
- use **sticky walls** at the correct moments,
- combine bouncing and wall-sticking in sequence,
- and avoid a newly introduced hazard: **lasers**.

Unlike the earlier rooms, mistakes in this room should have clearer consequences. This is no longer a safe tutorial space.

### Environment and Visual Style

The room should still maintain the sterile laboratory identity of the level, but it should feel larger, more industrial, and more dangerous.

- The chamber is **big, tall, and mostly empty**, with a strong sense of vertical space.
- The walls, floor, and ceiling still use laboratory materials such as white tiles, reinforced panels, and metal framing.
- The emptiness of the room should help the player focus on the traversal path.
- There should be a number of **platforms suspended or built into the room**, creating a route upward.
- The final goal is a **vent in the ceiling**, which serves as the exit.

The room should feel like a facility test chamber or vertical maintenance space, where traversal is the main challenge.

### New Hazard: Lasers

This room introduces **laser obstacles** for the first time.

- Lasers are dangerous security systems placed across parts of the traversal route.
- Touching a laser causes **instant death**.
- On death, the player **respawns at the start of the room**.

The lasers should be clearly visible and visually distinct from the rest of the environment.

They can be presented as:

- red security beams,
- thin glowing hazard lines,
- or scanning barriers embedded between platforms or across narrow routes.

This is the player’s first exposure to a true fail-state hazard, so the danger must be readable and fair.

### Layout Overview

The room should be built around a sequence of increasingly demanding traversal tasks leading to the ceiling vent.

#### 1. Entry Area

The player enters from the open doorway near the bottom of the room.

- This opening establishes the starting checkpoint for the room.
- From here, the player can see that the room is large and vertical.
- The vent in the ceiling should be visible or at least strongly implied, so the player immediately understands the destination.

The opening view should communicate:
**“I need to get up there.”**

#### 2. First Platforming Section — Re-establish Movement

The first section should ease the player into the room by asking them to move across a few platforms.

- These platforms should require controlled jumping or bouncing.
- The challenge should be more demanding than Room 2, but still manageable.
- This section serves as a warm-up before hazards are introduced.

This reminds the player that bounce-based traversal is now expected.

#### 3. Sticky Wall Section — First Vertical Route Change

At a certain point, the player reaches a platform that does not directly connect to the next one.

Instead, they must use a section of **sticky wall** to continue upward or sideways.

- The platforms should be positioned so the player can jump toward the sticky wall.
- The sticky wall should be visually marked with the same corroded yellow-green surface language established earlier.
- The player should climb along it to reach another ledge or platform.

This reinforces that climbing is not optional — it is part of the intended route.

#### 4. First Laser Introduction — Safe but Serious

After the player is comfortable moving through the room, introduce the first laser obstacle.

- This should be placed in a simple, readable position, such as across a platform path or between two ledges.
- The player should be forced to respond to it through timing, positioning, or choosing the correct approach angle.
- The first laser should be easy enough to understand, but dangerous enough to teach the consequence.

This moment should communicate:
**“This room will punish careless movement.”**

#### 5. Combined Traversal Section — Bounce, Stick, Avoid

The middle of the room should form the main challenge.

This section should require the player to:

- bounce to a platform,
- move toward a sticky wall section,
- climb or stick along it,
- and avoid lasers placed to punish poor positioning.

The route should be designed so that the player cannot simply brute-force movement. They need to understand the intended sequence.

Possible examples:

- a laser cuts across a direct jump path, forcing the player to bounce to a lower side platform first,
- a sticky wall segment leads past a laser that blocks a normal approach,
- or a bounce launches the player toward a wall section they must catch accurately.

This is the first room where movement mechanics and hazards are fully combined.

#### 6. Final Ascent — Ceiling Vent Exit

The last section should be the most demanding part of the room.

The player must complete a final upward traversal challenge to reach the vent in the ceiling.

This can involve:

- one final bounce to a high platform,
- a short sticky-wall climb near the top,
- and one or two laser placements that create tension without becoming unfair.

The vent should feel like a clear reward and endpoint for surviving the room.

Once the player reaches it, they escape upward and transition to the next room.

### Intended Difficulty

This room should feel noticeably harder than Room 2.

Key differences:

- The player is no longer just discovering mechanics.
- The room expects them to apply bounce and climbing with confidence.
- Mistakes are punished by death and reset.
- Hazard placement creates tension and precision.

However, difficulty should come from **clear mechanical execution**, not confusion. The room should still be readable, fair, and learnable.

### Respawn and Punishment

Because lasers cause instant death, the restart flow matters.

- The player should respawn at the start of the room.
- The route from the start should be short enough that failure is punishing, but not frustratingly slow.
- The room should avoid excessive repetition before the main challenge begins.

The punishment should create tension, not annoyance.

### Visual Communication

To make the room readable:

- Platforms should clearly indicate the intended route upward.
- Sticky surfaces should stand out strongly from ordinary lab walls.
- Lasers should be bright, obvious, and impossible to mistake for background decoration.
- The ceiling vent should serve as a visible goal.

The player should always be able to understand where they are trying to go, even if getting there is difficult.

### Summary of Room Identity

**Room 3 is a large vertical challenge chamber that combines bouncing, wall-sticking, and deadly laser hazards into the player’s first true test of movement mastery.**

Its progression role is:

- **Room 1:** learn climbing.
- **Room 2:** learn bouncing.
- **Room 3:** combine movement skills in a harsher, more dangerous traversal challenge.

### Intended Player Experience

The room should create the following sequence:

1. The player enters a large chamber and sees the vent high above.
2. They use platforms and bouncing to begin ascending.
3. They use sticky walls at key points to continue the route.
4. They encounter lasers and learn that contact means instant death.
5. They combine bounce, wall-stick, and hazard avoidance in a more demanding challenge.
6. They finally reach the vent in the ceiling and escape the room.
