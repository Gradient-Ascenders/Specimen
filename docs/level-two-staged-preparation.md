# Level 2 staged GPU preparation — 9 September 2026

The initial GPU preparation wait is substantially reduced. The final production sample has a 60 FPS median in every room and no multi-second gameplay frames, but occasional frames up to 400 ms remain. This does **not** establish the previous 117 ms worst-frame result or completely hitch-free gameplay.

## Method and evidence

Same Intel UHD 620 machine, Edge headless, 1280×720, minified Vite production build. The existing `?debug=1` enables authored Level 2. Room navigation is assisted through the debug panel; it is not an uninterrupted manual playthrough. No CPU sampler or WebGL wrappers ran during the production performance measurement. Automated tests/builds ran separately. Camera and timing differences affect moving drones and particles.

Ignored evidence is in `artifacts/performance-freezes/`: `load-before.log`, `preparation-before.ts`, `production-stable.json`, `production-stable-events.json`, `production-stable-preparation.json`, `production-stable-audit.json`, `stable-summary.json`, and `staged-final-tests.log`. Earlier iterations (`production-staged`, `production-local-layout`, `production-batched`, `production-variants`, `production-final`) document rejected versions, not the final result. The prior performance baseline is documented in `docs/remaining-performance-freezes.md`.

## Requested findings

1. **Cause of the approximately 95-second load.** The old helper fully prepared all five rooms and 13 overlapping room/light configurations before Room 1 could start. Most time was waiting for shader compilation, particularly Room 5 lighting and shadow combinations. Texture uploads and scene traversal were small contributors. Different visible light counts multiply Three.js shader variants even when materials are otherwise shared.

2. **Before timing breakdown.** Instrumented preparation across the 13 configurations:

   | Category | Time |
   |---|---:|
   | Asynchronous renderer/shader compilation wait | 83,231 ms |
   | Render priming, including first-use buffers/program finalization | 7,063 ms |
   | Additional shadow preparation | 2,718 ms |
   | Scene traversal | 218 ms |
   | Explicit texture GPU initialization | 25 ms |
   | Sum of measured preparation categories | 93,254 ms |

   The approximately 95-second transition also includes construction and transition overhead. Shader compilation wait is wall-clock elapsed time, not 83 seconds of JavaScript CPU. Geometry, materials and procedural maps are constructed synchronously within level construction, rather than regenerated by preparation. These nested costs must not be added again to the preparation total. A separate construction CPU sample is described below.

3. **Initial load before → after.** Approximately 95 seconds of whole-level preparation becomes **3,723 ms of initial GPU preparation**, plus **846 ms level construction** (approximately **4.6 seconds combined**). The previous small-layout iteration measured 2.8 seconds GPU preparation; timings vary between cold runs. Room 1 no longer waits for the remaining twelve configurations. The final journey recorder combines Level 1/Level 2 loading and scripted startup waits under one label, so that combined phase is not presented as Level 2 load time.

   Total staged elapsed work is approximately 181 seconds, including 152 seconds of asynchronous compiler waiting concurrent with play and scheduler yields. Total work becoming ready is **not** faster than the old eager preparation; the important change is that it no longer holds Room 1 loading open. Small compilation batches trade total throughput for responsiveness. The final accelerated route needed an 8.5-second protected wait between Room 4 and Room 5, then all thirteen configurations were ready. The route spends only about 30 seconds each in Rooms 1 and 2 and under 10 seconds in Room 3; it does not establish that normal puzzle play requires this fallback.

5. **Immediate resources.** The level's authoritative scene and gameplay are still constructed together. GPU preparation initially covers shared active presentation, Room 1 and its initial visibility configuration. This is GPU staging, not streaming or reconstructing gameplay rooms.

6. **Incremental resources.** Later room materials, their compatible shader variants, hidden future drawables, geometry buffers, and depth/distance shadow variants are prepared by one owned queue. Textures are initialized once per texture identity; geometry upload tracking is shared across configurations. Unused foundation/test-room drawables are excluded while their live lighting is retained. No assets are deleted.

7. **Scheduling and readiness.** Configurations are ordered along existing room visibility/progression boundaries, which begins later-room work early and uses the elevator window naturally. Background steps have a 1.5 ms scheduling budget and defer after frames over 20 ms; foreground fallback uses 5 ms. These budgets bound scheduling, not an uninterruptible WebGL call. Compilation runs through Three.js `compileAsync` in batches of eight. An unready current configuration becomes the queue's priority. The fallback overlay pauses fixed simulation, including both bodies and hazards, until the view is ready. Browsers without `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` defer preparation to the protected loading boundary. Failures remain stopped with an explicit retry/reload message.

8. **Shader correctness changes.** Preparation uses isolated proxy objects and material copies so live rendering cannot overwrite the material `currentProgram` which `compileAsync` is waiting on. It preserves custom shader hooks and cache keys, explicitly distinguishes ordinary meshes from instanced meshes, waits for both sides of two-pass transparent materials, and includes custom dissolve depth/distance shaders. These distinctions eliminated the intermediate approximately one-second instanced-marking stalls and multi-second Room 5 dissolve-shadow stall. Renderer target, viewport, scissor and shadow state are restored synchronously. Background priming does not replace gameplay draw counters.

9. **Variant count.** Approximately 349 programs in the preceding pass, or 352 with the updated platform/pod art in the instrumented eager baseline, become **221** after all configurations and completion. Ordinary non-shadow point counts use small compatible layouts (2, 8 or 17); Room 5 keeps at least 9 across its doorway/interior. Added slots have zero colour/intensity and cast no shadows. The seventeen-slot-everywhere experiment reached 111 programs but reduced moving-elevator performance, so it was rejected. The final version preserves the elevator’s two-light layout and Room 5’s nine-slot layout. Authored searchlights, illumination and shadow passes remain unchanged.

10. **Material reuse.** Existing live lab material instances remain owned and shared by the established material systems. Temporary preparation materials are deduplicated per material/geometry feature combination and retain Three.js-compatible cache keys, allowing the renderer to share compatible programs. Retained preparation materials keep programs resident until runtime teardown. There is no broad rewrite of room art materials.

11. **Procedural texture caching.** Existing generated lab, acid, sticky-wall, platform and warning maps remain shared and reused across rooms/resets. No new procedural texture generation or per-frame map work is introduced by the queue. No unnecessary additional cache or dependency was added.

12. **GPU texture upload.** Explicit initialization took approximately 25 ms before, 15 ms in the final staged sample: it does not explain the 95-second wait. Material maps range from 32 to 512 pixels in this audit, with about 14.2 MiB estimated RGBA8+mipmap data. No 4K replacements, downscaling or extra image downloads. This estimate excludes driver allocations, shadow/render targets and other overhead.

13. **Shadows.** Depth and point-distance shaders are compiled independently, using a one-pixel target and no full-room shadow render during background work. All nine authored 512px searchlight shadows and the 512px Volt point shadow remain. The recent Room 5 platform art had created room-wide shadow batches: it is now spatially batched into 10 m cells, with simple original-deck shadow hulls. Visible ceramic tops, bevels and underside fittings remain detailed; tiny underside fittings no longer individually cast shadows. This is a limited presentation trade-off, not a claim of pixel-identical shadows. Submitted Room 5 geometry fell from about 1.09 million triangles in the initial staged experiment to about 116,000 in the final first view.

14. **Median FPS.** All final room samples, including the first Room 5 view, measure approximately 60 FPS. Room 5: pre-collision-fix 30 → previous performance pass 60 → final staged pass 60. Level 1 remains 60. The entire Room 4 journey phase, including the moving elevator and waves, also has a 16.7 ms median; the rejected seventeen-slot version had a 33.3 ms median there. A separate fixed-camera comparison used identical 47 calls/5,042 triangles with and without padding. These are sampled medians, not a guaranteed minimum or locked 60 FPS.

15. **Fixed-update CPU.** Room 5 first sample: pre-fix 12.5 ms → previous pass 7.4 ms → final 6.6 ms. Final restarted samples are 5.9–6.4 ms, and extended Room 5 is 6.4 ms. Collision transform reuse and per-query ancestor-update caching are retained. Different sampled positions and encounter timing prevent treating the difference alone as a further collision improvement.

16. **Worst gameplay frame.** Pre-fix 9,564 ms → previous fully prepared pass 117 ms → final staged journey **400 ms**. Room-phase maxima are Room 1 117, Room 2 67, Room 3 250, Room 4 400 and Room 5 267 ms. These intervals include scripted navigation, screenshots and restarts. The largest recorded preparation step was 407.5 ms while priming the existing instanced wet death-droplet material in a future lighting configuration; the scheduling budget cannot preempt that driver call. Residual subsecond spikes are a regression against the earlier best worst-frame measurement and are not hidden by the 60 FPS median.

17. **Multi-second freezes.** No gameplay frame over one second occurred in the final assisted production journey, including the moving elevator, later-room entries, three restarts, a further 60-second Room 5 observation, rescue and completion. Loading is recorded separately. Level 1’s separate initial loading was not optimized here.

18. **Room-entry hitches/fallback.** The accelerated final journey recorded **8,481 ms** behind the preparation overlay between Room 4 and Room 5; its longest fallback frame was 50 ms. Both bodies/hazards pause safely. After readiness, Room 5’s first 90-frame sample had a 60 FPS median. Some noticeable subsecond hitches remain in room phases; completely hitch-free entry is not proven. Rapid skips and devices without parallel compilation can wait longer. No claim that the fallback will always be brief on every device.

19. **Resources and ownership.** GPU residency grows progressively, then stabilizes at **1,393 geometries, 45 textures and 221 programs** through the extended sample, all three restarts, rescue and completion. Initial Room 1 sample was 64 geometries/24 textures/18 programs. Counts are uploaded renderer resources, not gameplay mesh counts. Extra geometry reflects the latest platform/pod art and spatial batches; one texture is the preparation target. Map estimate remains 14.2 MiB. End audit: 681 level colliders, 517 Room 5 colliders, 16 acid surfaces; acid pool capacity 12; one session HUD listener, one runtime HUD listener, two Room 5 subscriptions. The final heap sample was approximately 110 MB; one sample is not a multi-hour leak proof. Queue-owned materials, instance proxies, target and overlay are disposed; borrowed geometry/textures remain with their original owners. In-flight compilation cleanup waits for completion.

20. **Visual quality.** Existing materials, texture sizes, props, acid effects, lighting intensities, fixture positions and shadow resolutions remain. Reviewed production screenshots are recorded below. The explicit exception is simplified small platform shadow detail from point 13; visible platform geometry is preserved.

21. **Gameplay.** Layouts, collision meshes, drone detection, acid rules, dissolve timing, checkpoints and reset contracts are unchanged. The only added simulation boundary is a pause while an unprepared view is protected by the loading overlay. Automated collider/LOS/reset invariants pass. Assisted production journey details are recorded below; scripted positioning does not substitute for a claimed complete manual parkour playthrough.

22. **Verification.** 398 automated tests passed, zero failures. Type-check and production build passed. Regression tests exercise isolated scene/material ownership, failed compilation, instanced versus ordinary variants, custom shadow variants, retained geometry ownership and unchanged authored lights. Production profiling reported no console/page errors or HTTP failures. `git diff --check` passed with line-ending advisories; no unresolved merge entries. No commit, push or PR.

23. **Remaining risks.** Subsecond frame spikes remain above the previous 117 ms result, particularly first-use wet death-fragment priming; strict imperceptible-stutter acceptance is not met. Background compiler time still competes for CPU/GPU capacity. The work budget cannot forcibly interrupt WebGL or a driver. The accelerated journey needed an 8.5-second fallback; unusually fast progression can outpace preparation, and unsupported browsers use protected foreground preparation. The test is a bounded single-device assisted route, not every camera/device or a multi-hour soak. Construction is still synchronous, roughly 0.85 seconds. Retaining shader variants trades some residency for stable first use.

## Construction CPU sample and production gameplay regression

The independent development CPU sample is in `loading-cpu.cpuprofile`, with `construction-breakdown.json`. Its construction wall time was 736 ms. Approximately 717 ms of sampled construction stacks split into 329 ms geometry creation/batching, 136 ms procedural texture generation, 18 ms material setup and 234 ms other construction/registration work. These are mutually exclusive, approximate stack classifications, not separately instrumented production timers. Geometry includes buffer conversion/merging; texture-generation stacks take precedence over their containing material constructors. Sampling misses some GC/native/unattributed time. There were no external image textures to download in this path. Construction code and texture generation were not changed by the light-layout adjustment.

The rapid Room 5 debug-skip test (`fallback-check.log`) verified identical Bob/Goop positions and checkpoint state across a one-second pending interval, a visible preparation message, and overlay removal on queue cancellation. The compile-failure path and borrowed-resource cleanup are also covered by automated tests.

The final production journey (`gameplay-stable.log`) passed Level 1 start, Level 2 entry, all five room visits, Room 3 acid contact/wake/landing, all 60 seconds of the elevator and 12 cable burns, Room 5 vent/sewer travel, three damaged-drone burns, red/blue/green network controls, Volt rescue/unlock, deliberate acid death and Retry preserving the rescued checkpoint, Volt terminal power and final completion. Three Room 5 restarts and a 60-second extended observation also passed. No console/page/HTTP errors were recorded. The final acid pool emitted 13 events over the journey and returned to zero active events within its fixed capacity of 12.

Body positioning and selected dissolve calls were scripted. This is a complete assisted state/transition journey, **not** a claimed uninterrupted manual puzzle/parkour playthrough. Collider, LOS, puzzle and reset semantics are additionally covered by the automated suite. Production elevator and Room 5 screenshots were inspected; existing art remained visually consistent.
